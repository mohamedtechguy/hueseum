About Hueseum

A gallery you enter by colour

Hueseum is a web application that searches a real museum collection by the dominant colour of each artwork. It was built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and it talks to three open APIs.

How colour matching works

The Art Institute of Chicago analyses every digitised image and records its dominant colour as three numbers:

Hue (H)
Position on the colour wheel, from 0° to 360°. Red sits at 0°, green near 120°, blue near 240°.
Saturation (S)
How intense the colour is, from 0% (grey) to 100% (pure colour).
Lightness (L)
How light or dark it is, from 0% (black) to 100% (white).

When you search, Hueseum converts your colour to the same three numbers and asks the collection for works whose hue falls inside your tolerance. If you choose to match tone, it also limits saturation and lightness. Colours with very low saturation, such as greys, browns and blacks, are matched on lightness alone.

Each result then gets a match score out of 100. Hue difference counts for 60% of the score, and saturation and lightness for 20% each.

The APIs behind Hueseum

  • Art Institute of Chicago API

    Search by colour, era, type and keywords. Full details for one work. Live check of which works are on display, and in which gallery. Images come from its IIIF image server.

  • The Color API

    Gives each colour you pick a human name, and suggests neighbouring colours to try next.

  • Wikipedia REST API

    Adds a short biography of the artist when you open a work.

Accessibility statement

Hueseum aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

  • Every page can be used with a keyboard alone. A "Skip to main content" link is the first thing you reach.
  • Colour is never the only way information is shown. Every swatch also has a name and a hex code, and match scores are written as numbers.
  • Artwork images use the museum's own descriptive alt text where it exists.
  • Form fields have visible labels, and errors are explained in words next to the field.
  • Search results and status messages are announced to screen readers.
  • Text and controls meet a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 in both lighting modes.
  • Animations are switched off if your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Body text uses Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers.

Questions

Why do some results look a different colour from the one I picked?

The museum measures the single most common colour in an image. A painting can be mostly blue sky with a small red boat, and it will be listed as blue. Raise the tolerance or turn off "Match tone" for broader results.

Where is my exhibition saved?

In your browser's local storage, on this device only. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing your browser data removes it.

Can I reuse the images?

Works marked "Public domain" are released by the Art Institute of Chicago under a CC0 licence. Others are shown for study only. Tick "Public domain works only" on the Explore page to see reusable works.

Is the route card a ticket?

No. It is a planning aid. Check opening times and buy admission on the Art Institute of Chicago website.
